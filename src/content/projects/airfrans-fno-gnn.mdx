## The problem

During my Master's I used Ansys Fluent to simulate building environments, aircraft cabins and human respiration. Some models had millions of cells and took days to solve, and changing a single boundary condition meant solving again. Simulation is grounded in real physics, but once enough simulations exist, a data-driven surrogate can return a new prediction in a fraction of the time.

This project predicts the velocity, pressure and turbulent viscosity fields around an airfoil directly from its geometry and the inflow conditions, using the [AirfRANS](https://github.com/Extrality/AirfRANS) dataset (800 training and 200 test simulations of 2D incompressible RANS flow).

## Two approaches

- **Fourier Neural Operator (FNO):** interpolates the mesh onto a regular grid, learns in truncated Fourier space, then interpolates back to the mesh.
- **Graph neural network (GNN):** an encode-process-decode network in the style of MeshGraphNet, working directly on the point cloud through a k-nearest-neighbour graph. No grid needed.

## Results

<SurfaceErrorChart />

| Model | Parameters | Training (50 epochs) | Inference per simulation |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| FNO | 4.34M | ~35 min (Apple MPS) | ~1.9 s |
| GNN | 5k | ~8.2 h (CPU) | ~0.7 s |

The GNN wins every channel, both over the whole field and at the surface. The gap is widest at the surface, where the no-slip boundary layer lives.

## Why the FNO hits a floor

The FNO has to move data from mesh points to a grid and back. I measured the error that round trip alone causes, before training any model, by passing the true fields through it:

| Grid | vx, all points | vx, surface |
|---|--:|--:|
| 128×64 | 49.3% | 117.9% |
| 256×128 | 32.3% | 100.7% |
| 512×256 | 22.1% | 81.0% |

Higher resolution helps the far field but barely moves the surface error. CFD meshes are densest near the wall, exactly where a regular grid throws the most information away. A density-weighted loss helped three of four channels but could not close the gap.

## What didn't work

I tried a no-slip penalty on the GNN, penalising predicted velocity at wall nodes. It made wall velocity more than twice as bad (17.4 to 42.4 m/s mean), and larger weights made held-out accuracy worse. All four outputs share the same message-passing layers, so a loss on two channels at about 0.5% of nodes interferes with the shared representation instead of fixing the wall.

## Limitations and next steps

- Single train/validation/test split, so no error bars.
- The GNN uses a k-NN graph rather than the true mesh connectivity, and has no edge features.
- Next: rebuild the graph from the mesh triangulation with edge features, try per-channel loss weighting, and test a geometry-aware FNO.
